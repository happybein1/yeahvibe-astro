## Loneliness is a signal, not a flaw

Loneliness is the feeling that the contact you have is not the contact you need. Like hunger or thirst, it is a signal that a basic need is not being met.

It is not about how many people are around you. You can feel lonely in a crowd, in a family or in a relationship, and feel fine with only a few close friends. What counts is the quality of the connection: feeling seen, understood and able to count on someone.

Almost everyone feels lonely at some point, often after a move, a break-up, a new job, retirement or a loss. It is nothing to be ashamed of.

## Why it matters

Short periods of loneliness are part of life. Long-term loneliness is different: it affects mood, sleep, stress and even physical health, about as much as other well-known health risks. It can also make people more cautious and withdrawn, which makes reaching out harder. That is why small, early steps matter.

## Small steps that work

You don't need to change your life to start. Try one of these this week:

- **Send one message** to someone you've lost touch with. "I was thinking of you, how are you?" is enough.
- **Say yes to one invitation**, even if part of you would rather stay home.
- **Have short chats**: with a neighbour, a colleague, the person at the shop. These small moments add up more than people expect.

## Places where connection grows

Friendships rarely come from a single meeting. They grow from seeing the same people again and again, doing something together. Look for regular, shared activities:

- a club, choir, sports team or walking group;
- volunteering for something you care about;
- a class or course;
- a local community group or neighbourhood project.

Pick one, go at least three or four times, and let it build slowly.

## Deepening what you have

Connection is not only about meeting new people. The relationships you already have can often become closer:

- **Listen fully**, without planning your answer.
- **Ask real questions**: "How are you really doing?" rather than "All good?"
- **Show up when it counts**: a message on a hard day, help with a move, remembering what matters to someone.
- **Share a little of yourself.** Closeness grows when both people open up.

## When it's heavier

If loneliness comes with low mood that doesn't lift, hopelessness or dark thoughts, contact a doctor or a helpline in your country. You don't have to sort it out alone.

## At the societal level

How easy it is to meet people depends a lot on how society is organised: the design of neighbourhoods, public spaces and transport, working hours, the ageing of the population, and how much of life has moved online. Places where people can meet regularly and for free, like libraries, parks, community centres and clubs, are one of the simplest ways to reduce loneliness for everyone.
