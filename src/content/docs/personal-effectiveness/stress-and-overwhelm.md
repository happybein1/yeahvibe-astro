## Stress isn't the enemy

Stress is your body getting ready to act. Your heart beats faster, your muscles tense and your attention narrows. In short bursts this helps: it gets you through a deadline, an exam or an emergency.

The trouble starts when it never switches off. When the pressure is constant, the body stays on alert day after day, and that slowly wears you down. The goal is not to get rid of stress, but to give it an end.

## Signs you're overloaded

Overload often shows up before you notice it in your thoughts:

- **In the body**: poor sleep, headaches, a tight jaw or shoulders, tiredness that rest doesn't fix.
- **In the mind**: racing thoughts, forgetting things, finding it hard to decide or concentrate.
- **In behaviour**: snapping at people, putting things off, scrolling to escape, dropping things you enjoy.

If you recognise several of these, it is a signal to act, not a sign of weakness.

## Calm it in the moment

These take about two minutes and work almost anywhere:

1. **Breathe out longer than you breathe in.** In for 4, out for 6, for ten breaths. A long out-breath tells the body it is safe.
2. **Name the feeling.** "I'm anxious about the meeting." Putting it into words makes it smaller and easier to think about.
3. **Move.** A short walk, even around the room, burns off some of the tension.

## Lighten the load

Calming down helps, but the load is still there. To reduce it:

1. **Empty your head.** Write down everything on your mind: tasks, worries, small things. All of it.
2. **Sort it** into four groups: *do now*, *plan for later*, *drop*, and *ask for help*.
3. **Pick three things for today.** Only three. The rest is written down, so it can wait without being forgotten.

Doing this once brings relief. Doing it every morning or evening keeps the pile from building up again.

## Protect your recovery

Stress becomes harmful when there is no recovery. Guard the basics: [sleep](/personal-effectiveness/sleeping-well/), some movement every day, time away from screens and notifications, and time with people you trust. If worry is what keeps you stuck, the guide on [managing fear](/personal-effectiveness/fear-management/) can help.

## When to get help

If stress lasts for weeks, affects your sleep, work or relationships, or comes with low mood or panic, talk to a doctor or someone you trust. Asking for help early is a smart decision, not a failure.

## At the societal level

Much of today's stress has shared causes: heavy workloads, insecure jobs and housing, rising costs and constant digital demands. Personal habits help, but lasting relief also depends on how work, cities and services are organised, and on whether people have enough security to plan their lives.
