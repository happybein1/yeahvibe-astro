## First, breathe

Money worries are among the most common sources of stress, and they are nothing to be ashamed of. They often come from things outside your control: a lost job, an illness, a separation, rising prices.

Stress also makes it harder to think clearly. That is why the first step is not to fix everything at once, but to get a clear picture and take one step at a time.

## See the full picture

Take one quiet hour and write down, on one page:

- **What comes in** each month: salary, benefits, any other income.
- **Fixed bills**: rent or mortgage, energy, water, insurance, phone, transport, with their due dates.
- **Debts**: who you owe, how much, and when payments are due.

It can feel scary to look. But once it is on paper, the problem has a size and a shape, and it usually feels smaller than the worry in your head.

## Protect the essentials first

When there isn't enough for everything, pay in this order:

1. **A roof over your head**: rent or mortgage.
2. **Energy and water.**
3. **Food.**
4. **Health insurance and anything that would stop you from working**, like transport to your job.

Then look at debts. Put first those with the biggest consequences, such as eviction, disconnection or court action, not simply the ones that call the loudest.

## Talk to creditors early

Most landlords, energy companies and lenders would rather agree on a payment plan than chase unpaid bills. Contact them before you miss a payment if you can, explain your situation, and offer what you can realistically pay. Don't ignore letters: problems that are ignored usually grow, with extra costs on top.

## Find help you may be missing

Many people don't receive support they are entitled to. Check:

- **Benefits and allowances** for housing, children, health care or low income.
- **Free debt advice.** Many countries and cities offer free, confidential help to sort out debts and talk to creditors for you.
- **Local services**: social services, community groups, food banks.

Avoid paid "debt solution" offers and quick loans with high interest. They often make things worse.

## Small steps that give you room

- **Cancel what you don't use**: subscriptions, memberships, extra insurance.
- **Use a weekly amount** for daily spending, and note what you spend so you can see what is left. The Budget tab in [ShopTools](https://grocery.yeahvibe.com/) does this for you.
- **Shop with a list** and plan meals for the week.

None of these solve everything, but together they give you some breathing room and a sense of control.

## When to get help

If your debts keep growing, you can't pay for essentials, or the worry affects your sleep or your health, talk to a debt adviser and to a doctor or someone you trust. You don't have to sort it out alone.

When things are stable again, the guide on [money basics](/personal-effectiveness/mastering-finance-basics/) will help you build savings and plan ahead.

## At the societal level

Money problems are rarely only personal. Rising housing and energy costs, insecure work and easy access to expensive credit put many households under pressure at the same time. Fair wages, affordable housing and easy-to-reach support make a big difference to how many people end up in debt.
