## You have a plan. So why isn't it happening?

You've chosen what matters and made a clear plan. And yet the task sits there, day after day. This is not laziness. Putting things off is usually a way to avoid an unpleasant feeling: boredom, doubt, fear of doing it badly, or not knowing where to begin.

The good news: you don't need more motivation. Motivation usually comes **after** you start, not before.

## Why we put things off

- **The task is too big or too vague.** "Work on the project" gives your brain nothing to grab.
- **Fear of doing it badly.** If it can't be perfect, it feels safer not to start.
- **The reward is far away.** The effort is now, the benefit is in months. Scrolling rewards you right away.
- **Too many choices.** When everything is possible, nothing gets chosen.
- **Low energy.** Sometimes the honest answer is that you're tired (see [Sleeping Well](/personal-effectiveness/sleeping-well/)).

Ask yourself which one it is. Naming the reason already makes it smaller.

## Make the first step tiny

Shrink the task until it feels almost silly:

- Not "write the report", but **"open the file and write the title"**.
- Not "get fit", but **"put on your shoes"**.
- Not "sort out my finances", but **"find last month's bank statement"**.

A tiny step is hard to refuse. Once you've started, continuing is much easier than starting.

## The two-minute rule

Tell yourself: **"I'll do it for just two minutes."** You're allowed to stop after two minutes. Most of the time you won't, because the hardest part, starting, is behind you.

## Make it easy to start, hard to avoid

- **Decide when and where.** "Tuesday at 9, at the kitchen table" works much better than "this week".
- **Prepare the night before.** Leave the file open, the shoes by the door, the papers on the table.
- **Remove the easy escape.** Phone in another room, notifications off, one tab open.
- **Tell someone.** Saying "I'll send it by Friday" to a friend or colleague adds a gentle push.

## Be kind when you slip

Everyone puts things off sometimes. Blaming yourself adds a bad feeling, which makes you want to avoid the task even more. Instead, notice it, without judging, and ask: "What's the smallest next step?" Then take it.

## When it's more than putting things off

If you can't start anything for weeks, even things you used to enjoy, it may be low mood or exhaustion rather than a planning problem. [Check in with how you feel](/personal-effectiveness/check-in-with-how-you-feel/), and talk to someone you trust or a doctor if it lasts.
